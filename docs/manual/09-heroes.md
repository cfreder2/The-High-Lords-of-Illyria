# Heroes, Ruins and Temples {#heroes}

::: lore
*"Sir Gawen went into the Weeping Shrine with a lantern and a borrowed sword. He came out with neither, and with the Crown of Kings on his head."*

— the news, day 31
:::

Heroes are what make the war more than a contest of production. A hero leads a stack, and makes every army in it fight a little better; carries artifacts that make it fight a great deal better; and, above all, **explores**. The land is full of places a hero may search: ruins with their guardians and their treasure, temples that bless armies, and two sages who know where the treasure lies.

## Heroes {#hero-rules}

Every lord begins with one hero, its lord, named for its capital (*Lord of Kingshold*), in one stack with an escort of light infantry, the realm's own rare troops and the capital's guard of archers and heavy infantry. More heroes come by **hire** (below). A hero:

- is an army of one man, strength **3 + his level** (and a point for each battle artifact and blessing, never past 9);
- rises a level with kills, in any fight: 3 kills for level 2, then 7, 12, 18, 25, 33, 42 and 52 for level 9. Each level is 6% more damage and 20 more hit points (chapter [](#hero-levels-in-battle));
- **leads**: in a fight every soldier of his side within 30 m of him has the *hero leads* edge (+2% at strength 4 to 6, +4% at 7 or 8, +6% at 9), and the computer lords reckon the same for every army of the best hero's stack (chapter [](#edges));
- carries any number of **artifacts**, which go where he goes and fall where he falls;
- draws no upkeep;
- **never breaks** in battle (chapter [](#morale)).

A hero who dies in the campaign is gone for good (in Horde he comes back between the waves: chapter [](#hero-returns)). What he carried lies where he fell, and the next hero of any side whose stack marches through that cell takes it up ("*Dame Rowena took up the Sunblade*").

## Searching {#searching}

Select a stack of yours standing still on a ruin, a temple or a sage, and a button at the bottom of the screen offers **Search** (a ruin), **Visit** (a temple) or **Consult** (a sage), with the place's name. Ruins and sages need a hero in the stack (*Only a hero can search here*); the strongest hero in it goes in. A temple will bless any stack.

## Ruins {#ruins}

There are 34 ruins in Illyria: 33 in the forests and the foothills, with names like the *Sunken Halls*, the *Weeping Shrine* and the *Howling Keep*, and the **Drowned Abbey**, on its isle in the Heartmere, reached on foot along the Spit. Each holds a **guardian**, and the hero goes in **alone** to fight it. This is the one fight of the war that is thrown with dice, the *Warlords* way: each round a ten-sided die each; a roll no higher than the *other's* strength is low; when one rolls low and the other high the low roller takes a hit; two hits and it is over. Nobody else in the stack takes part, and it is done in a moment.

| Guardian | Strength |
|---|---|
| a pack of wolves | 2 |
| a band of brigands | 3 |
| a troll | 4 |
| the restless dead | 5 |
| a giant spider | 5 |
| a minotaur | 6 |
| a demon | 7 |
| an ancient wyrm | 8 |

Guardians are fiercer the further the ruin lies from the nearest capital of a lord in the war: a young hero finds an easy ruin near home and a hard one in the wilds. Within about 900 m of a capital a ruin holds one of the first four of the table; beyond about 1.8 km, anything from the restless dead to a demon. (The ancient wyrm is in the rules, but no ruin of this map lies far enough out to hold one.) A hero of strength 4 against a troll (4) is a coin toss; against brigands (3) he wins two times in three; against wolves (2), about four times in five.

**A hero who falls** in a ruin is gone, and what he carried lies in the ruin for the next hero; the guardian waits on. **A hero who wins** gains the guardian's strength in kills (and maybe a level), and finds one of these:

- **Gold** (four times in ten): 50 + 25 for each point of the guardian's strength + up to 50 more; wolves leave 100 to 150, a demon 225 to 275.
- **An artifact** (a little over a third of the time): one that has not yet been found. Weak guardians (strength 3 or less) guard only moderate treasures (+1, the Sandals and the Purse among them), middling ones (4 or 5) up to considerable ones (+2), the fiercest anything up to the supreme (+3); when none of the fitting kind is left, any that is. When all fifteen have been found, there is gold instead.
- **Allies** (a quarter of the time; gold instead if the stack already has eight armies): undead behind the weakest guardians (strength 3 or less); undead or wizards behind those of 4 or 5; devils or archons behind the minotaur; a dragon or demons behind the demon. Allies join the hero's stack and want no pay. Two bands come at once of the undead behind a guardian of strength 5 and of the devils, if there is room for both.

Each ruin is searched once; after that it lies empty for good (the button says so).

## Artifacts {#artifacts}

Fifteen artifacts lie hidden in Illyria, each unique, all in the ruins. **Battle** artifacts add to the hero's own strength. **Command** artifacts add whole points to the strength of every army of his stack, as the *Warlords* command modifier did. Two more serve the road and the purse.

Strength is the figure on a card and the figure the computer lords reckon with (chapter [](#dice)). In the fight itself each point of a battle artifact is a **blessing** on the hero, and each point of a command artifact a blessing on **every man of his stack**: 10% more damage, blows 7% quicker and 6% less hurt taken, each (chapter [](#blessings-in-battle)).

| Artifact | Kind | Worth |
|---|---|---|
| Firebrand | battle | +1 |
| Frostblade | battle | +1 |
| Spear of Ashkar | battle | +1 |
| Sunblade | battle | +2 |
| Shadowblade | battle | +2 |
| Bow of the Morning Star | battle | +2 |
| Staff of Thunders | battle | +3 |
| Crown of Kings | command | +1 |
| Sceptre of Dawn | command | +1 |
| Scarlet Banner | command | +1 |
| Ring of Dominion | command | +2 |
| Horn of the North | command | +2 |
| Rod of the High Lords | command | +3 |
| Winged Sandals | the road | the hero's stack is reckoned a third quicker on the march (see below) |
| Purse of Plenty | the purse | 10 gold a day |

A hero's card names what he carries and the command points he gives his stack (hold the pointer over it).

::: note "The Winged Sandals in this edition"
The Sandals shorten the march as it is planned: the times you are shown, the route chosen, the computer lords' sums. The men themselves, who since the one world walk every metre of the way on their own feet, do not yet walk any faster for them.
:::

::: tip
A command artifact is worth more than a battle one: the Crown of Kings blesses every man in the stack, the Sunblade one hero twice. A hero with the Rod of the High Lords and seven armies of light infantry beside him leads eighty-four men with three blessings each: 30% more damage, blows a fifth quicker and nearly a fifth less hurt.
:::

## Temples {#temples}

Four temples stand in Illyria, one in each quarter of the land: the **Temple of the Dawn**, the **Temple of the Tides**, the **Temple of the Stars** and the **Temple of the Oak**. As in *Warlords*, a temple blesses **any stack**, hero or none: every army in it that has not knelt there before gains a **blessing**, +1 strength (shown ✦ on its card). An army can carry a blessing from each temple, four at most, and its strength is never reckoned past 9. A blessing is felt in every fight: 10% more damage, blows 7% quicker, 6% less hurt taken, and steadier nerves (chapter [](#blessings-in-battle)).

Each temple also has its **guard**, holy troops who follow the **first hero** to come and kneel there, blessed and serving for nothing:

| Temple | Its guard |
|---|---|
| The Temple of the Dawn | archons |
| The Temple of the Tides | two bands of journeymen |
| The Temple of the Stars | unicorns |
| The Temple of the Oak | pegasi |

The guard comes blessed by its own temple. It needs room in the stack: a stack of eight armies gets none, and the guard waits for the next hero; a stack of seven at the Temple of the Tides gets one band of journeymen and the other is lost. The computer lords reckon a stack defending a temple or a ruin's stones (+4%, the *holding* edge) a little stronger; the fight itself gives it nothing (chapter [](#edges)).

## Sages {#sages}

Two sages live in Illyria, each in a strange place: the **Seer's Tower** on the Isle of the Seer off the north-west coast, and the **Hermit of the Peaks**, high in the Ironveil, reached on foot by its pass. Nobody walks to the isle: a hero comes by boat and lands on its shore (chapter [](#boats)), or is hired in the fishing village that is the isle's only place, if you hold it. The first hero to consult each sage is given **100 to 150 gold** and told the name of the nearest ruin still unexplored. First come, first served: the second finds that the sage *has spoken*.

## Heroes for hire {#hire}

Now and then, at dawn, a hero offers his sword to a lord who holds **more than two places for each hero** it already has (one chance in twelve each day). He waits in one of your places, chosen at random, and asks **150 to 200 gold, and 100 more for every hero already in your service**, paid now. One time in three he brings a band of rare troops with him (griffins, pegasi, unicorns, wizards, berserkers or journeymen), who serve for nothing, and asks 50 gold more.

The offer appears as a prompt: **Hire for 270 gold** or **Decline**. It stands until the next dawn. You cannot hire while an enemy is raising a banner over the town where he waits, nor if you have lost it. He joins a stack of yours standing there, or makes his own. A lord whose heroes have all fallen is offered another in time (with no hero, one place is enough). The computer lords hire when they can pay the price and keep 100 gold over. The heroes of Illyria are sixteen, Sir Aldric, Dame Isolde, Brannoc the Bold, Lady Mirelle, Torvald Ironhand, Elowen Swiftbow, Kaelan the Grey and Ulfric Bearsbane among them; when all sixteen have offered, the next are their namesakes, *the Younger*.
