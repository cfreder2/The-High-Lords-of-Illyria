# Blood, Voices and Sound {#blood}

::: lore
*"Casualties, my lord."*

— the herald

## How a soldier dies {#death}

How a man dies decides what is left of him and how it sounds. Every weapon has a kind of damage: **slash** (swords, sabres, claws), **cleave** (axes, great blades, a fiend's or a dragon's claws), **blunt** (clubs, staves, an elephant's weight, a catapult's stone), **pierce** (pikes, lances, fangs, horns, arrows), **explosive** (satchel charges) and **fire** (fireballs, Molotovs). The killing blow's kind, the way it came from, and its **overkill** (how far below nothing it took him) choose the body:

| The killing blow | The body | Left behind |
|---|---|---|
| Slash past 12 overkill, cleave past 7 (a man-sized body) | whole but for a limb; past twice that, the head | the part, thrown on along the blow, bleeding as it skids; a pool where he fell |
| A blast past 5 overkill | torn apart | head, trunk, limbs and worse, flung from the blast, rolling down slopes too steep to grip, leaving trails of blood |
| Anything but fire and arrows, past 50 overkill (a catapult stone landing on a man) | torn apart | as above, burst from the side the blow came from |
| An arrow | whole, fallen the way the arrow flew | the arrow, pinned in him where it struck |
| Fire | whole, burned black | smoke rising from him for a while |
| Anything else | whole, fallen the way the blow drove him | |

A blast throws its pieces long and low: each strikes the ground, hops on and skids to a stop, leaving a splat of blood at every strike and a smear along the skid, so a burst leaves red trails radiating out from a ragged black star of scorch. Loose pieces can be kicked by men walking through them. On a castle's wall-walk the dead lie along the walk, never across it into the stone. In a skirmish or a match the dead of both sides stay where they fell for the rest of the battle, the corpses and the arrows in the ground; nothing is tidied away. In the campaign, where the battle never ends, the dead lie for ten minutes at most and are then taken away. The undead and catapults do not bleed: the dead leave bones, the engines a heap of timbers.

## The voices {#voices}

As in *Myth*, the soldiers speak. Fourteen voices (the soldier, the archer, the dwarf, the rider, the hero, the wizard, the berserker, the archon, the engine crew, the wordless brute, the undead, the fiend, the beast, and the herald who reports your losses) have lines for forty-four situations, about a thousand lines in all.

- **Answering orders.** Selected, a soldier answers: "*Sir?*", "*Orders?*", "*Bow ready.*", "*Aye? What now?*" (a dwarf). A hero answers for those he leads.
- **The battle as it goes.** A veteran's call to hold the line, a hero's rally, a hero's fall on either side ("*The lord is down!*"), breaking ("*Ride for your lives!*"), wavering and rallying ("*Back to it.*"), kills and a veteran's taunts ("*Stay down.*", "*Another feather.*"), promotions, bad wounds, arrows falling, blows in the flank, friendly fire, a grenadier's Molotov ("*Fire in the hole!*"), healing, the catapult throwing, a gate broken ("*The gate's down!*"), first sight of the enemy, riders breaking into the gallop and the men they ride at, victory and defeat.
- **Idle chatter** after twenty seconds without a blow: "*My feet ache.*", "*Need more arrows.*", "*I need a drink.*"
- **The cries** of pain and of death, and the choking moan of a man shot, by the way he died.
- **The herald**: "*Casualties*" when a quarter of your soldiers have fallen, "*Severe casualties*" at half, "*Total casualties*" at four fifths. In Horde and Capture the Flag (chapter [](#other-wars)) he also calls the news of the match: a wave coming, a boss wave, a wave beaten, reinforcements come up, the hill in the horde's hands, your flag under attack, your flag lost, an enemy flag taken.

The enemy uses the same lines, a little lower. Voices are heard from where the camera looks, fading over 140 m; orders, victory, defeat and your own hero's fall are always heard. They take turns: grave news cuts in, chatter waits. Lines not yet recorded are read by your device's own speech voice (or, for the wordless, a short growl) if you allow it in the settings.

## Sound {#sound}

There is no music in battle, as in *Myth*: every sound tells you something.

- **The fighting:** bowstrings; Molotovs thrown and shattering into flame; fireballs; catapults loosing; arrows landing in earth, wood, stone or water, or on what they hit (a shield's splintering crack, armour's ring, the wet thwack of flesh); blows by their kind and the armour they meet; blasts, stones, gates struck and broken; and the deaths.
- **Arrows passing** close to a low camera whistle, higher as they come.
- **Ambience:** the wind (the high passes howl; the lowlands sweep, louder as the camera rises), insects and frogs near marsh and mud, carrion flies over the dead a little after they fall, and the enemy's **war drums** while he comes on beyond bowshot (from 300 m, louder as he nears), silent once the armies meet.
- **The matches' alarms:** a war horn for each wave of the horde, and an alarm when the hill or your flag is in danger.
- **Victory** gets a fanfare, **defeat** a drone.

Sounds fade with distance from the camera, and with its height.

## The settings {#settings}

**Settings** on the title screen (and ⚙ in play) has four pages: Graphics, Sound, Gameplay and Controls. The first two are about what this chapter describes. They are kept on the device, and none of them changes the battle: everyone sees and hears the same fight on any setting.

### Graphics {#settings-graphics}

Three presets, and each part of the look on its own. Changing a part by hand leaves the presets.

| Setting | What it draws | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ground detail** | grass, soil, flowers and stones painted into the ground, and the shade under the trees | | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Grass and ground cover** | tufts of grass and plants standing on the ground | | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Dense ground cover** | more of it, and further from the camera | | | ✓ |
| **Sun shadows** | the shadows of men, trees and walls | | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Sharp, wide shadows** | crisper shadows, reaching further | | | ✓ |
| **Detailed distant land** | the far hills and woods in full | | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Sky, haze and clouds** | the sky, the haze of distance and the clouds | | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Film look (bloom, grade, grain)** | a glow round bright things, a grim colour grade, darkened corners and a fine grain over the picture | | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Full resolution** | every pixel the screen has: sharpest, slowest | | | ✓ |

A phone starts on Medium, a computer on High. Two more switches stand outside the presets: **Dynamic resolution** draws the picture coarser when the frame rate drops and finer when it recovers (it has nothing to do while Full resolution is on), and **Show frame rate** shows the frames a second and where a frame's time goes.

### Sound {#settings-sound}

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Master volume** | everything at once |
| **Soldiers' voices**, and their volume | the voices of [](#voices), on or off |
| **Read unrecorded lines aloud** | lets your device's own speech voice read the lines not yet recorded |
| **Sound effects**, and their volume | the sounds of the fighting |
| **Ambience**, and its volume | wind, swamp, flies, drums and rain |
| **Test sounds** | opens the **sound test**, where every sound and cry in the game can be heard on its own |
