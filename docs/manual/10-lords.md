## The computer lords {#computer-lords}

The seven lords you do not play are played by the computer. As the *Warlords* manual promised of its computer players, there are **no secret bonuses**: they see the whole map, as you do, and play by exactly your rules, your prices and your dice. When a campaign begins you choose how well they play.

### Easy: the Knights

*Slow, careless, short of gold.* Like the *Warlords* Knight, which "deliberately wastes money and sometimes forgets what it is doing", an Easy lord:

- loses a fifth of its income;
- forgets a stack one day in four, and picks a target at random one time in three;
- attacks at odds as poor as 30 in 100;
- sees an attack coming only when it is at the gates;
- trains whatever takes its fancy (the quickest army when the enemy is at the gates);
- never gathers its stacks together, sends recruits to the front, never raises its walls and does not mind its veterans;
- hears of a banner going up over one of its towns only at dawn.

### Hard: the Warlords

*Every trick, no mercy.* The computer's lords default to Hard. A Hard lord:

- works out its **exact** chance of winning each battle, state by state through the dice, with every modifier and edge you would have;
- keeps in each city as many guards as it takes to hold off the worst threat in reach (green common troops first), and pulls stacks back to save a city when they can get there first;
- strikes your stacks in the field near its cities when the odds are good;
- gathers its free armies into strike stacks of up to eight, strongest first, siege engines in the first, and attacks the best prize for the risk within four and a half days' march, at odds of 40 in 100 or better (45 with a hero along, and more for each veteran or rare army it would risk: a hero, a veteran is worth more than a town);
- joins a small stack to a bigger one nearby when together they can take what neither could alone;
- leaves its veterans and rare troops at home whenever the attack would still have the odds without them;
- trains what fights best for the days it takes (an army today beats a better one next week), a few catapults for the walled towns of the later war, and stops training before its upkeep outruns its income;
- raises the walls of its frontier towns when it has gold to spare;
- sends its heroes after ruins they can beat, temples and sages, when no enemy is near; takes every levy it holds; hires heroes when it can pay;
- builds boats (three at most) when a city it could take is out of reach on foot or much quicker by sea, and sails stacks of three or more at it;
- answers at once when you raise your banner over one of its towns;
- late in the war, **gangs up on the leader**, which may be you.

::: note "Not yet built"
*Warlords* gave its computer players hatreds: attitudes to each of the other empires, which made them leave some alone and fall on others. The lords of Illyria have none yet; they gang up on whoever leads instead.
:::

## How to win {#victory}

You win the war in either of two ways, as in *Warlords*:

1. **Be the last lord standing.** A lord is out of the war when it holds no city and has no army anywhere.
2. **Make them surrender.** When one lord holds **more than half** of the eighty cities (41 or more) and no other lord holds within 15 of its number, the rest lay down their arms.

When you win, the field says *High Lord of Illyria*: "The other lords of Illyria lay down their arms before you." When another lord does, *Illyria is lost*.

## Strengths and weaknesses of the realms {#realm-hints}

Each lord's start shapes its war. The computer lords play their realms this way, and so may you, when you can choose your side.

**The Aurelian Throne (slow to start, strong to finish).** Kingshold is in the middle of everything, with neutral towns on every side and every other lord within a fortnight's march. The heavy cavalry are the best field army in the land and the catapults the key to every capital; the pegasi are the only flier you start with, worth a point to every army in the stack. Take the towns round Kingshold fast, before your neighbours do, and hold the crossings of the Aldwater.

**The Highcrag Giants (slow and tough).** The poorest chest and only foot to train, but the Greymere is a moat round the whole north-west: only Greymere Bridge and Greymere Ford lead in. Take the north-west, then the crossings, then come down when you are ready. Two giants and the levy's third make a stack that walls cannot stop for long.

**The Ironhold Dwarves (take to the hills).** The richest chest in Illyria, the dwarven legion (strength 5, the best common foot), catapults, griffins and grenadiers. The dwarves are slow everywhere but the hills. Expand fast in every direction; the plains of Valdor are next door, and so are the Riders.

**The Orcs of Grath (down and dirty).** Wolf riders are fast (14 points a day), strength 5, and at home among the trees in battle; warriors are the best sword-and-shield foot. Two levies of berserkers wait in Grathmaw. The eastern spurs are a strong position with few roads in; take the Eastreach towns quickly before the Order wakes up.

**The Sylvaran Elves (learn to hate).** Archers that walk the woods at road speed (forest costs them 2), light cavalry, unicorns and pegasi. Take the forest towns first; the Aldwater's fords are your front.

**The Sunreach Riders (fast and loose).** Light cavalry cover 18 cells of road a day. Make every town of the Valdor plains yours before anyone else can walk there, then turn on the Dwarves or the Throne. Mind the woods: they slow horse to a crawl.

**The Moonward Order (patience).** Wizards (strength 7, a special army: +1 to every army beside them) and journeymen, archers and pikemen; and Moonhaven sits by the Heartmere at the centre of the east. Strong defenders, slow attackers. Let the others bleed each other at the crossings.

**Lord Malvoren (slow and safe).** In the far south-east, with mountains behind and the Cinder Spur beside him, Malvoren is the last to be attacked. Spiders are quick (12 points a day, and the marsh costs them only 4), and Malvoren's armies like the marsh. Build slowly, take the south-east, and choose your enemy.
