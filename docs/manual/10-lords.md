# The Other Lords, and Victory {#lords}

::: lore
*"The Warlord does not hate you. You are simply standing on his road."*

— Kaelan the Grey
:::

## Choosing the war {#setup}

A new Conquest (**Conquest**, a slot, **New game**) asks three things before day 1.

- **Who will you be?** Any of the eight lords. You take that realm's capital, its hero, its treasury, the kinds it trains and its rare troops (chapter [](#realm-hints)).
- **Which lords will you face?** Any of the other seven, from all of them to none; **All** and **None** set the lot. A lord you do not face is *not in the war*: no hero, no army, nobody playing him. His capital stands as a neutral walled place with a garrison of five armies, and the places of his country are neutral like any others.
- **How well should they play?** **Easy** or **Hard**, for all of them alike. The one offered first is the one set in Settings, Gameplay (Hard, until you change it).

**Raise your banner** begins the war. The choice is part of the saved game and cannot be changed afterwards. With no lord against you there is nobody to beat: the land and its neutral places are yours to take, and the game has no end.

## The computer lords {#computer-lords}

The lords you face are played by the computer. As the *Warlords* manual promised of its computer players, there are **no secret bonuses**: they see the whole map, as you do, and play by exactly your rules and your prices, and their men fight as yours do, blow for blow. Each gives its orders **once a day, at dawn**; between dawns its stacks march and fight on what they were told. (One thing wakes a Hard lord at any hour: a banner going up over one of its towns.)

Before a lord attacks, it works out its chance with the old dice rules (chapter [](#dice)). That sum decides whether it marches. It decides nothing about the fight.

### What every lord does

On either difficulty a lord:

- leaves at least its weakest army in each of its places when the rest march, green common troops first; heroes and catapults do not stay behind as guards unless nobody else is there;
- when an enemy is in reach, keeps home as many guards as its sums say will hold him off, and everything when that is not enough; pulls back stacks that can get there before the enemy, the richest place (and the capital above all) first; and does not throw armies after a place it reckons lost;
- sends each free stack at the best prize for the risk within **four and a half days' march**, never at worse odds than it will take, and never with fewer than a third as many men as the defenders;
- races for the neutral places for the first thirty days, and after that weighs the enemy's towns, and his capital most of all, more heavily;
- stays in a town while its banner rises there;
- sends its heroes, when no enemy is near, to search ruins whose guardian is weaker than the hero, to temples and to sages within about a kilometre; a hero in a town slips out alone and leaves the others on guard;
- hires a hero when it can pay him and keep 100 gold over;
- keeps its armies' pay within 95% of its income (and a twentieth of its chest), trains nothing it could not keep, and sends its weakest armies home before the chest runs dry;
- builds boats (three at most, one at a time) where a place it could take is out of reach on foot or reached in under three fifths of the time by sea, and sails stacks of three armies or more at it, leaving one army to hold the port.

### Easy and Hard, in numbers

| | Easy: the Knights | Hard: the Warlords |
|---|---|---|
| Income | loses a fifth of it | all of it |
| Attacks at reckoned odds of | 30 in 100 or better | 40 in 100 or better |
| ... with a hero in the stack | 30 in 100 | 45 in 100 |
| ... for each veteran, elite or rare army it would risk | nothing more | 6 more, to 85 in 100 at most |
| Thinks a place of its own in danger when the enemy's chance of taking it is above | 65 in 100 | 40 in 100 |
| Sees an enemy marching on it, or idle in the field, from | half a day's march | two days' march |
| Minds an enemy sitting in his own town | within a day's march | not until he marches |
| Forgets a stack for the day | one day in four | never |
| Picks a target at random, not the best | 35 times in 100 | never |
| Trains | whatever takes its fancy (the quickest, with an enemy at the gates) | what fights best for the days it takes |
| Hears of a banner over one of its towns | at the next dawn | at once |

### Easy: the Knights

*Slow, careless, short of gold.* Like the *Warlords* Knight, which "deliberately wastes money and sometimes forgets what it is doing", an Easy lord has the numbers above and none of the Warlord's habits below: it never gathers its stacks for a purpose, never sends its recruits to the front, does not strike at stacks in the field and does not spare its veterans.

### Hard: the Warlords

*Every trick, no mercy.* Besides its numbers, a Hard lord:

- **sallies**: a garrison that is threatened marches out and destroys the threat itself, all but one guard, when the odds are good; and its stacks strike enemy stacks in the field that threaten its places, within about half a kilometre;
- **gathers**: a stack too weak to attack alone marches to join a bigger one in a town of its own within a day and a half, when together (eight armies at most) they could take what neither could alone; and a stack with reinforcements a day off waits for them unless the prize is all but sure;
- **spares its best**: a stack setting out from one of its towns leaves its veterans and rare troops at home, the strongest first, whenever the attack would still have the odds without them;
- **sends recruits forward**: quiet towns in the rear send what they train to the town nearest the next prize;
- **builds catapults** from day 40, about one for every eight places it holds, in frontier towns that can build them;
- **answers a banner at once** (chapter [](#banners));
- **gangs up on the leader**: once one lord holds twelve places or more and half as many again as the next, every Hard lord values his towns above all others. That lord may be you.

::: note "Not yet built"
*Warlords* gave its computer players hatreds: attitudes to each of the other empires, which made them leave some alone and fall on others. The lords of Illyria have none yet; they gang up on whoever leads instead. Nor does any lord, you included, raise a town's walls in this edition.
:::

## How to win {#victory}

You win the war in either of two ways, as in *Warlords*:

1. **Be the last lord standing.** A lord is out of the war when, at a dawn, it holds no place and has no army anywhere. A lord with one army left and no town is still in it.
2. **Make them surrender.** When one lord holds **more than half** of the eighty places (41 or more) and no other lord still in the war holds within 15 of its number (the nearest is 16 or more behind), the rest lay down their arms.

When you win, the field says *High Lord of Illyria*: "The other lords of Illyria lay down their arms before you." When another lord does, *Illyria is lost*: "Another lord has won Illyria, and the rest bow to him." The clock stops there. In a war with no lord against you (see [](#setup)) neither can happen.

## Saving the war {#saving}

A Conquest lives in one of **three slots**, kept in this browser on this device.

- **It saves itself** into its slot when it begins, at every dawn (Settings: *Autosave every day*), and when you go back to the menu.
- **💾 Save** in the menu saves now, into a slot you choose; from then on that is the slot it saves into.
- **A save is the whole war:** every man where he stands, with his wounds, his kills and his nerve; every gate's damage, banner, hex and treasury; the orders waiting behind each man; who is in the war and how well they play. It keeps nothing of the moment: arrows in the air are gone and the dead are cleared away, so a fight saved half-way goes on from where the men stand.
- Clearing the browser's data clears the slots.

## Strengths and weaknesses of the realms {#realm-hints}

Each lord's start shapes its war. The computer lords play their realms this way, and so may you, whichever you choose. Every realm has ten places drafted from its own country round its capital, walled towns and villages (chapter [](#continent)), and fights 8% harder and marches 15% quicker on its own kind of land (chapter [](#edges)).

**The Aurelian Throne (slow to start, strong to finish).** Kingshold is in the middle of everything, with every other lord a march away. Its own country is the open fields and the roads. The heavy cavalry are the best field army a town can train, and the catapults the key to every wall; the pegasi are the only flier you start with, and go over a wall while the rest are still at the gate. Take the places round Kingshold fast, before your neighbours do, and hold the crossings of the Aldwater.

**The Highcrag Giants (slow and tough).** The poorest chest (200 gold) and only foot to train, but the Greymere is a moat round the whole north-west: only Greymere Bridge and Greymere Ford lead in. The hills are theirs. Take the north-west, then the crossings, then come down when you are ready. Two bands of giants march with the lord and the capital's levy raises a third: nine giants of 350 hit points each.

**The Ironhold Dwarves (take to the hills).** The richest chest in Illyria (450 gold), grenadiers to train (Molotovs and satchel charges), catapults, and two flights of griffins from the first day. Deepforge is in the far south-west. Grenadiers are slow (1.8 m/s) and lose less of it on bare rock than anyone; the hills are the Dwarves' country. Expand fast in every direction; the Elves in the Longwood are the nearest lord.

**The Orcs of Grath (down and dirty).** Wolf riders are the fastest thing a town trains (7.5 m/s), strength 5, and nearly as quick among the trees; warriors are the best sword-and-shield foot. A minotaur band and berserkers march with the lord, and two levies of berserkers wait in Grathmaw, in the north-east. Fens and hills are the Orcs' country. Take the Eastreach towns quickly before the Order wakes up.

**The Sylvaran Elves (learn to hate).** Archers that walk the woods almost as fast as the open fields, light cavalry, unicorns and pegasi. Sylvaris stands in the Longwood, in the south, and the woods are the Elves' country: fight there. Take the forest places first, and mind the Dwarves to the west and Malvoren to the east.

**The Sunreach Riders (fast and loose).** Light cavalry ride 1.7 km of road in a day, and the open country is the Riders' own. Sunreach stands in the west, between the Greymere and the Westrun. Make every place of the western plains yours before anyone else can walk there, then choose between the Giants beyond the Greymere and the Throne to the east. Mind the woods and the fens: horse goes at half pace in the one and a third in the other.

**The Moonward Order (patience).** Wizards (a strength of 7 in any lord's sums, and a special army: a point more for every army beside them when he reckons whether to attack you), journeymen who heal, archers and pikemen; and Moonhaven sits by the Heartmere at the centre of the east. The water's edge and the woods are the Order's country. Strong defenders, slow attackers. Let the others bleed each other at the crossings.

**Lord Malvoren (slow and safe).** Dreadspire stands in the south-east, toward the Sorrow. Spiders are quick (5 m/s, and nearly as fast in a forest), and the fens and the woods are Malvoren's country. Heavy infantry, wolf riders and catapults to train. Build slowly, take the south-east, and choose your enemy.
