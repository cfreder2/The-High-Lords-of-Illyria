# A Compendium of Troops {#compendium}

::: lore
*"Know what you are sending, and what you are sending it against."*

— the drill book of the Grey Watch
:::

Every kind of army in Illyria, one to a page: the common troops the cities train, the beasts and monsters of the land, the allies of the old powers, the specialists of the old wars, the hero and the engine.

## Reading the entries {#reading-entries}

Each entry has a picture of one soldier of the kind, in your colours, and a card of his numbers. These are read from the game's own unit sheet every time this book opens, so they are always the game's.

- **Strength** is the *Warlords* number on the army's banner (chapter [](#strength)).
- **Hit points**, **speed** (metres a second on open ground), **height** and **mass** (a man is 1). Mass decides who gives way when bodies meet and how hard a charge lands.
- **Blow**: the damage of one blow, its kind (chapter [](#death)), and how often he strikes; **reach**, how far.
- **Shield**: the chance a blow from the front is turned. **Knockback**: how hard his blows throw a man.
- **Morale**: how hard shocks shake him, against a man's (lower is steadier; chapter [](#morale)).
- **Missile**, **range**, **flight** and **gait**, where he has them (chapter [](#rules)).

Under the card: what the army is, what it costs in the campaign, and how to use it.

- **Who trains it.** Every place trains light infantry. An open **village** trains nothing else. A **walled town** adds one or two kinds after the land it stands in (hills: grenadiers, heavy infantry, pikemen; woods: archers, wolf riders, warriors; plains: heavy infantry, light cavalry, pikemen, heavy cavalry), so "hill towns" below means *some walled towns in the hills*. A **capital** trains its realm's own kinds, at twice the pace: the days given are a town's, and a capital needs half of them.
- **Rare troops** are never trained. A **levy** is a band a place holds and musters once (three days, and five gold a point of strength): each capital holds its realm's, and about one other place in twelve holds one after its land.
- **"In the dice"** means in a computer lord's reckoning of a fight before he attacks (chapter [](#dice)); the fight itself is fought, not rolled.

::: unit Light Infantry | light_infantry
The most common army in Illyria: spearmen and swordsmen in leather and a little mail, lightly armed for speed and poorly trained. They are the cheapest troops and the quickest to train, every city, town and village raises them, and they make the backbone of every garrison and every first expedition.

**Campaign.** 12 men. 2.4 m/s on open ground. Trained everywhere: 2 days, 12 gold, 3 a day.

**On the field.** Light infantry die quickly against anything better, and shake easily (110% of a man's shocks). Use them to hold a line in numbers, to screen your archers, and to stand in the courtyard: a flagpole counts men, not quality. Beside one veteran they hold much longer.
:::

::: unit Heavy Infantry | heavy_infantry
Men in mail and plate behind big shields, with swords: the line of battle. Their shields turn more than half the blows that come at them from the front, they are slow, and they are steady.

**Campaign.** 12 men. 1.9 m/s on open ground. Trained in hill and plain towns, and at Kingshold, Highcrag, Deepforge and Dreadspire: 4 days, 20 gold, 5 a day.

**On the field.** The shield wall. Hold them in **Line** facing the enemy and they will take a charge and give it back; turn them, or let them be caught side-on, and the shield is no use at all. Guard their flanks with pikes or horse.
:::

::: unit Archers | archers
Young men with longbows, unarmoured and light on their feet. In the woods they move as other men move on a road. Their arrows reach seventy metres, further from a height, and every one is a real arrow that may hit what it was aimed at, or something else.

**Campaign.** 12 men. 2.4 m/s on open ground, and nearly as fast in a forest. Trained in woodland towns, at Sylvaris and Moonhaven: 4 days, 20 gold, 5 a day. They have the familiar-ground edge in forest (+4%).

**On the field.** Keep them **Loose** (their default), on high ground if you can, behind a line, and away from anything that charges: in melee they are almost helpless. Stop shooting into a melee your own men are in, or accept the cost. Give them a ridge and they will outshoot archers on the flat who cannot reach them.
:::

::: unit Pikemen | pikemen
Foot with long pikes, the answer to horse. Their reach (2.2 m, nearly four times a swordsman's) lets them strike first, and their points do two and a half times as much (+150%) to anything mounted.

**Campaign.** 12 men. 2 m/s on open ground. Trained in hill and plain towns, at Highcrag and Moonhaven: 4 days, 20 gold, 5 a day. Half the villages' own levies are pikemen: a neutral village may meet your horse with a hedge of points.

**On the field.** Standing still and facing the charge, pikemen are **braced**: riders who charge their front run onto the points, deal nothing and are thrown back, badly hurt (chapter [](#pikes)). Marching, or taken in the flank, they are just men with long sticks. Halt them facing the horse; put them in a **Double line** across a ford or a gate. They fight best in the open (+3%).
:::

::: unit Light Cavalry | light_cavalry
Riders in leather on fast horses, with sabres: among the quickest armies on the map and on the field. Their charge is not as heavy as the knights', but they come from anywhere.

**Campaign.** 8 riders. 7 m/s on open ground: on a road, 1.7 km a day. Trained in plain towns, at Sylvaris and Sunreach: 5 days, 28 gold, 7 a day.

**On the field.** Ride round. Their charge into a back is worth twice their charge into a front (80 against 40), and archers, wizards and engines cannot answer them. Never charge braced pikes; keep out of woods and bogs, which slow horses to a walk. **Wedge** is their default. They fight best in the open (+3%).
:::

::: unit Heavy Cavalry | heavy_cavalry
Knights in plate on barded horses, with lances: the hammer of the Throne and the Riders. A charge of heavy cavalry over thirty metres of flat ground nearly doubles their lance's blow and throws men off their feet.

**Campaign.** 8 knights. 5.5 m/s on open ground. Trained in plain towns, at Kingshold and Sunreach: 6 days, 36 gold, 9 a day.

**On the field.** Give them room to gather speed, flat or downhill, and a target that is not facing them. Uphill their charge is a quarter of itself; into braced pikes it is a disaster. Once in a melee they are strong but not special; pull them out with a force march, wheel, and charge again. Best in the open (+5%).
:::

::: unit Wolf Riders | wolf_riders
Goblins on great wolves, quick, vicious and at home among the trees, where horses falter.

**Campaign.** 8 riders. 7.5 m/s on open ground, the quickest on the map, and quick in a wood. Trained in woodland towns, at Grathmaw and Dreadspire: 5 days, 28 gold, 7 a day.

**On the field.** Cavalry for the woods: they keep most of their speed under the trees and charge out of them. Lighter than horse, they throw men less, but they come in fast and from the side.
:::

::: unit Giants | giants
Twelve-foot giants of the northern heights with clubs like tree trunks. A giant's blow knocks soldiers flying, and he strides through mud and fords that slow men to a crawl.

**Campaign.** 3 giants. 2.4 m/s on open ground; a marsh slows them less than men. Rare: two march with the Highcrag lord, Highcrag holds a levy of one more, and some hill towns have a levy of two. Upkeep 4 a day.

**On the field.** A giant is a battering ram on legs: 350 hit points, 45 a blow. He is big enough to be hit by everyone at once, and slow to turn: surround him and he dies. Use giants to break a line, or to wade a ford no one else can hold. They are tall enough to swat fliers out of the air.
:::

::: unit Minotaurs | minotaurs
Bull-headed monsters with great axes, from the labyrinths of the east. Not as big as giants, quicker, and nearly as hard-hitting.

**Campaign.** 4 minotaurs. 3 m/s on open ground. Rare: one marches with the Orcs' lord and one with Malvoren's; some hill towns have a levy of two. Upkeep 4 a day.

**On the field.** Heavy enough to turn slowly, like riders, so strike them from the side. In their own hands they cleave through light infantry like wheat.
:::

::: unit Giant Spiders | spiders
Spiders the size of ponies from the eastern woods, fast on eight legs and quick in the trees, with a venomous bite.

**Campaign.** 6 spiders. 5 m/s on open ground, and nearly as fast in a forest. Rare: Malvoren's lord has them from the start, Dreadspire holds a levy of two, and some woodland towns have a levy of two. Upkeep 3 a day.

**On the field.** Fast and many, low and wide; they come through woods at speed and their bites pierce. Fragile beside the other monsters; use them to run down archers and stragglers.
:::

::: unit Griffins | griffins
Half eagle, half lion, ridden by the dwarves' wing of scouts. Griffins fly over walls, rivers and mountains and fall on whatever lies behind.

**Campaign.** 4 griffins. On their own they fly at 4 m/s in the long run, over mountains and water alike; with walkers they walk at 3 m/s. Rare: two march with the Dwarves' lord from the start, Deepforge holds a levy of one more, some hill towns have a levy of two, and heroes for hire sometimes bring them. Upkeep 8 a day. A flier adds a point to every army in its stack in the dice, where they also count as at home in hills; on the field they walk as men do and have no ground of their own.

**On the field.** They walk until sent more than about fifty metres, then run, take off and fly, tire and must land. They never strike from the air: fly them behind the enemy line, land, and tear into the archers or the engines.
:::

::: unit Pegasi | pegasi
Winged horses with riders, the messengers of the old throne. Faster than griffins in the air, lighter in a fight.

**Campaign.** 4 pegasi. On their own they fly at 4.7 m/s in the long run; with walkers they walk at 4.5 m/s. Rare: the Throne's lord and the Elves' lord have them from the start, Kingshold holds a levy of one more, some plain towns have a levy of two, the Temple of the Oak's guard is pegasi, and heroes for hire sometimes bring them. Upkeep 8 a day. A flier: +1 to every army in the stack in the dice, where they also count as at home in forest; on the field they have no ground of their own.

**On the field.** Mounted: they can charge when they land at speed. Use them for the gate's far side, the courtyard, the archers on the hill.
:::

::: unit Elephants | elephants
War elephants with a howdah and crew, brought over the sea long ago to the Valdor plains. The largest creatures on the field but one, they trample and toss whole files of men.

**Campaign.** 2 elephants. 3.2 m/s on open ground. Rare: the Riders' lord has them from the start, Sunreach holds a levy of one more, and some plain towns have a levy of one. Upkeep 6 a day.

**On the field.** 450 hit points, 38 a blow and a knockback that throws men aside; a charge of elephants (they are mounted, and charge as riders do) is a terrible thing. But they are skittish (120% of a man's shocks): when they break, they break hard. Keep them away from blasts and fire, and in front of your line, not behind it.
:::

::: unit Unicorns | unicorns
White horses with a single spiralled horn, swift as light cavalry and fiercer, who come to the call of the elves.

**Campaign.** 4 unicorns. 7 m/s on open ground. Rare: the Elves' lord has them from the start, Sylvaris holds a levy of one more, some woodland and plain towns have a levy of two, the Temple of the Stars' guard is unicorns, and heroes for hire sometimes bring them. Upkeep 7 a day.

**On the field.** Their horn pierces, their speed is a horse's, and they are steady (70%). They are not mounted, so they do not charge as riders do: use them like very fast, very hard-hitting foot.
:::

::: unit Wizards | wizards
Mages of the old learning, who gather light in their hands and throw it as fire. A fireball bursts where it lands and burns everyone near.

**Campaign.** 3 wizards. 2 m/s on open ground. Rare: the Order's lord has them from the start, ruins give them as allies, and heroes for hire sometimes bring them. Upkeep 10 a day (allies serve for nothing). A special army: +1 to every army in the stack in the dice.

**On the field.** Fragile (45 hit points) and slow to reload (9 s), but a fireball into a close-packed line kills several and breaks the rest. Keep them **Loose**, behind the line, and well away from riders. They can strike at the men on a wall.
:::

::: unit Dragons | dragons
The ancient wyrms of the deep places, which guard the fiercest ruins and sometimes follow the hero who beats one. A dragon flies, breathes fire and kills anything it reaches.

**Campaign.** 1 dragon. On its own it flies at 4.9 m/s in the long run; with walkers it walks at 2.5 m/s. Rare: found only in ruins, behind the fiercest guardians. A flier and a special army: +2 to every army in its stack in the dice. Strength 9.

**On the field.** 650 hit points and a cleaving bite of 40; fireballs that burst five metres wide. Steady (25%) and slow on the ground. A dragon wins battles alone, but it is one creature: everything on the field will shoot at it.
:::

::: unit Devils | devils
Hulking fiends from below the mountains, red-skinned and horned, who serve whoever freed them from their ruin.

**Campaign.** 3 devils. 3.2 m/s on open ground. Rare: found only in ruins. A special army: +1 to every army in the stack in the dice.

**On the field.** Almost fearless (20% of a man's shocks) with a cleaving blow of 36. They walk into a line and stay there.
:::

::: unit Demons | demons
Greater fiends, bigger than devils and worse.

**Campaign.** 2 demons. 3.4 m/s on open ground. Rare: found only in ruins, behind the fiercest guardians. A special army: +1 to every army in the stack in the dice.

**On the field.** 320 hit points, a blow of 40 and reach to match. What an elite soldier always leaves alone (chapter [](#target-down)).
:::

::: unit Undead | undead
The restless dead of the barrows, raised by old curses. They do not tire, do not bleed and do not fear.

**Campaign.** 12 dead. 1.6 m/s on open ground, and as fast in a marsh. Rare: found in ruins, behind the weaker guardians. A special army: +1 to every army in the stack in the dice. They have the familiar-ground edge (+4%) in marsh, mud and shallows.

**On the field.** Slow and weak one by one, but they **never break**: they come on until destroyed. At home in the swamp. Their deaths leave bones, not blood.
:::

::: unit Archons | archons
Winged warriors in white, the servants of the heavens, who guard the Temple of the Dawn.

**Campaign.** 3 archons. On their own they fly at 3.7 m/s in the long run; with walkers they walk at 2.8 m/s. Rare: found in ruins, and the Temple of the Dawn's guard follows the first hero to kneel there. A flier: +1 to every army in the stack in the dice.

**On the field.** Strong (26 a blow), steady (30%), and winged. The best fliers to land in a courtyard.
:::

::: unit Hero | hero
A named champion: a lord of the realm, or a sword for hire. A hero leads, explores and fights like a squad on his own, and never runs.

**Campaign.** 1 hero. 2.8 m/s on open ground; he walks, so with fliers he keeps them on the ground. Every lord starts with one; others are hired (chapter [](#hire)). No upkeep. Strength 3 + his level, plus his battle artifacts.

**On the field.** Strength grows with his level (+6% damage and +8% hit points a level). He steadies everyone within 30 m, and calls the broken back. If he falls, those near him may break at once. Lead from the second rank.
:::

::: unit Dwarven Grenadiers | dwarf_grenadier
The one dwarf of Illyria: demolitionists with bottles of burning oil to throw and five satchel charges each to lay. (The old dwarven legion is no more; its men carry satchels now.)

**Campaign.** 6 grenadiers. 1.8 m/s on open ground; climbing slows them half as much as men. Trained in hill towns and at Deepforge: 4 days, 24 gold, 6 a day. They have the familiar-ground edge (+4%) on rock.

**On the field.** A Molotov (19 m at most) bursts where it strikes into fire three metres wide (40, burning the dead black). A laid satchel (80, four metres wide, three times as hard on a gate) lies still until a blast or fire reaches it, then sets off the next. Left alone they lay satchels in the path of whoever comes at them, step back, and throw fire at the satchels when the enemy stands on them. A grenadier killed by fire or a blast with satchels still on him goes up with all of them at once: keep them **Loose**, and away from your own line. Raiding hyena-folk steal laid satchels and throw them back, bursting where they strike.
:::

::: unit Journeymen | journeyman
Healers of the Moonward Order, with staves and a few precious charges of healing.

**Campaign.** 4 journeymen. 2.3 m/s on open ground. Rare: the Order's lord has them from the start, Moonhaven holds a levy of two, the Temple of the Tides' guard is two bands of them, and heroes for hire sometimes bring them. Upkeep 3 a day.

**On the field.** Each goes to a wounded friend within 25 m and mends 35 of his hit points, every 2.5 s, six times in a battle. They are no fighters: keep them **Loose** behind your heavy infantry.
:::

::: unit Warriors | warrior
Plain soldiers with sword and shield, the orcs' foot and the woodland towns' militia. Their shields are big and turn blows from the front, never from behind.

**Campaign.** 12 men. 2.2 m/s on open ground. Trained in woodland towns and at Grathmaw: 4 days, 24 gold, 6 a day.

**On the field.** Better than heavy infantry at strength (5), with the biggest shields of the common foot (65%). Face them at the enemy.
:::

::: unit Berserkers | berserker
Wild men who fight without shields and without fear, faster than any other foot and hitting harder.

**Campaign.** 10 berserkers. 3.6 m/s on open ground. Rare: the Orcs' lord has them from the start, Grathmaw holds a levy of two more, some woodland towns have a levy of two, and heroes for hire sometimes bring them. Upkeep 3 a day.

**On the field.** 25 a cleaving blow every second, at 3.6 m/s. They take only 35% of a man's shocks and keep coming. With no shields, arrows hurt them: close quickly. **Loose** is their default.
:::

::: unit Catapults | catapult
A wheeled mangonel with its crew, the only engine of war in Illyria. Slow on the road, useless in a melee, and the one thing that makes a walled capital fall.

**Campaign.** 2 catapults. 0.8 m/s on open ground, a quarter of that in a marsh: they want roads. Trained at Kingshold, Deepforge and Dreadspire: 6 days, 40 gold, 10 a day. In the dice, strength 3 in the field, 5 against a city, and each one takes a point off the walls' bonus; in the fight itself what counts is its stones on the gate.

**On the field.** Throws a stone 30 to 150 m that smashes everything within 3 m, and does five times its damage to a gate: four stones square on the doors break a town gate (chapter [](#gates)). Keep it well back, guarded; it cannot fight anything that reaches it.
:::

::: unit Hyena-folk
Hunched, laughing scavengers of the wastes with long scimitars, who care nothing for their own lives. Let loose to raid, they prey on whatever is weak: they snatch up what lies on the field and throw it at the stragglers and the soft men behind your line.

**Campaign.** Not in the campaign: nobody trains them and nothing gives them. They fight in the sandbox (chapter [](#sandbox)) and with the Red Hand at Oakhaven Ford (chapter [](#skirmish-ford)).

**On the field.** Weak in a stand-up fight (35 hit points, an 8 blow) but fast (6 m/s): only riders catch them, and they never break. They hold where they are sent until you order them to **Raid** (chapter [](#raid)); then, knowing only what they see 35 m about them, they take the best thing in sight, a satchel charge before a blade, a blade before a head, and dart in to throw it at archers, healers, wizards, grenadiers, the wounded and the straggler, when the pack is strong enough for the men about him, then fall back for the next. A severed head they throw breaks the man it strikes. They keep clear of armoured men and, while there is other prey, out of archers' reach. Against them keep your men together and facing them (a shield facing a throw catches it), and let your riders hunt them.
:::
