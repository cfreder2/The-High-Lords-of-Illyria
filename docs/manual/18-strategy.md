# Strategy {#strategy}

::: lore
*"Wars are won in the counting-house and lost at the ford."*

— the Moonward Order's maxims
:::

These remarks assume you are playing against Hard computer lords; Easy ones, and most people, are more forgiving.

## On the map {#strategy-map}

**Open villages first.** The first fortnight of the war is a race for the seventy-two neutral places, and every lord is running it. Fifty-one of them are open villages: no wall, no gate, and one levy on the green (light infantry or pikemen). Your lord's stack walks over a village on the first day. Each pays only 8 to 12 gold a day and trains only light infantry, but six of them lie in your own country and together they pay about as much as your capital. Take them before a neighbour does, and give each something to train the day its banner goes up.

**Then the walls at the crossings.** The twenty-one walled towns stand at the fords and bridges and at the feet of the passes, three in each realm's country. Each pays 21 to 30 gold a day, trains a kind or two the villages cannot, and is held by three to five armies: bowmen on the wall first, then foot behind the gate. A walled town is a siege (chapter [](#sieges)): do not walk a lone stack up to its gate. Come with the gate's price in hand (grenadiers and their satchels, a catapult if your capital builds them, or fliers to go over) and with more armies than the garrison, because your banner rises only when no army but yours has a man inside the wall. Whoever holds the walled towns holds the roads between the realms.

**Mind the pikes on the green.** Half the villages' levies are pikemen, and a rider who charges the front of pikes standing still deals nothing and is thrown back hurt (chapter [](#pikes)). The Riders and the Elves most of all: look at a village's levy before you ride at it. Send the foot at its front, or bring the horse round to its flank or back, where a charge goes home as usual.

**The capital is worth five villages.** It pays 50 gold a day, it alone trains your realm's own kinds, and it trains at twice a town's pace: two days' drill every day, so light infantry in one day and knights in three. Keep it training from the first day to the last, and send what it trains to the front (below).

**Never leave the capital empty.** The neighbours are racing too, and one enemy army inside your wall with none of yours there starts a banner. You begin with a guard of archers and heavy infantry in your lord's stack: leave them at home when he marches, the bows on the wall and the shields behind the gate, and train behind them.

**Raise banners with enough men.** A lone army takes about a minute, half a day, to raise a banner; eight take a third of that. An army of anyone else's that comes inside the place stops your banner while it stands there, more armies than yours bring it down, and if your men leave or fall the attempt is lost. Bring three or four armies, or follow up with more.

**Fight in your own country.** Each realm strikes 8% harder and marches 15% quicker on its own kind of land, anywhere in Illyria: the Throne and the Riders in the open, the Giants and the Dwarves in the hills, the Orcs in fens and hills, the Elves in the woods, the Order at the water's edge and in the woods, Malvoren in fens and woods (chapter [](#own-country)). Choose the ground of a fight when you can: meet the Riders in a wood and the Elves on the plain. The small edges together never pass 20%, and none of them makes a weak army strong.

**Keep the veterans alive.** An elite army is not much stronger in the dice than a green one (9%), but it cannot be bought, and in 3D it rallies first, steadies the green men near it and calls the broken back. Do not send veterans to garrison the interior, and do not throw them at walls: send green troops first, and the veterans when the odds are good.

**Rare troops are irreplaceable.** Every griffin, every giant, every journeyman you lose is gone for the whole war. Use them where they matter most: a flier in a stack is worth a point to every army in it; a special army another.

**Garrison lightly, and think about upkeep.** Every trained army costs a quarter of its price every day. Four light infantry cost 12 gold a day: about half of what a walled town pays you, and more than any village does. A village in the interior needs one army (3 gold of its 8 to 12) or none; a walled town on the frontier is worth two or three, bowmen among them for the embrasures. *Warlords*' rule of thumb still holds: a small garrison will not stop a determined attack, but it guards against cheap ones, and a single army inside the wall is enough to keep a passing enemy's banner from going up.

**Walls and gates.** A walled town's stone does not break, but its gate does, and nobody hangs a new one: a gate broken in the Conquest lies open for the rest of the war, and a gate hacked half through keeps its wounds. Break a gate and you inherit the hole: a town you stormed needs more men behind its gateway than one you took by drawing the garrison out, and going over on wings, or catching the doors open, leaves you a town with its gate whole. Each capture also lowers the town's *walls number* by one (never below 1). Only the computer lords read it, when they reckon whether to attack: a town that has changed hands often looks an easier prize to them. A village has no walls to count on at all: hold it with men, or expect to take it back.

**Send new armies to the front.** Rear cities should send what they train to the city nearest your next prize (**Send new armies to…**), two days later and with no marching. Up to four cities can feed one.

**Bring engines to the capitals.** A capital has an outer wall and an inner bailey, each with its own gate (2,000 and 1,500 hit points). Four catapult stones square on the doors break the outer gate; satchels laid at it and lit do the work without an engine (chapter [](#gates)). Only the Throne, the Dwarves and Malvoren build catapults, at their capitals; the others must bring grenadiers from a hill town, take a capital that builds engines, or go over on wings. Taking a capital is worth it: its 50 gold a day and its double pace become yours.

**Heroes should always be doing something.** Ruins near home have weak guardians: send your lord after them early, alone if you dare. A hero of strength 4 beats wolves four times in five and a troll one time in two. Visit the temples (every stack may), consult the sages (the first hero takes the gold), and hire heroes whenever you can afford them. Watch for an enemy hero alone or lightly guarded: you never know what he is carrying.

**Watch the leader.** Late in the war the Hard lords gang up on whoever leads. If that is you, expect it, and keep your frontier strong.

**The land.** A hex held gives the home-land edge (+3%) in every battle fought there. Hold the outposts at the crossings: an outpost claims the hexes round it, and a defender at one fights with the holding edge.

## On the battlefield {#strategy-battle}

**Pause and look.** Before you give an order, pause and look at the ground: where the hills are, where the woods and the fords are, where the roads go. Most battles are won before they start.

**Hold the high ground and make them climb.** Height is a small edge to every blow (up to +4%) and throws men harder downhill; your archers shoot a metre further for every metre they stand above their mark; the men who climb to you arrive slower; and a charge uphill is as little as a quarter of itself, where yours down the slope can be doubled (chapter [](#high-ground)).

**Face the enemy.** A line struck in the flank takes half as much again from every blow, in the back twice as much, and loses its charge, its shields and its nerve. Use the hold-and-drag to set a facing; use **Line** for foot and keep its flanks covered, by pikes, by horse, by a wood or a river.

**Flank them.** The cheapest victory is a charge into the back of a line already locked in melee. Light cavalry ridden round a flank are worth twice their charge into the front.

**Break them.** A soldier killed is one man; a soldier broken takes his squad with him. Kill their hero, and green troops near him break where they stand. Keep yours alive, behind the first line, within 30 m of as many of your own as you can.

**Mix your veterans in.** A green line holds half again as long beside one elite, and a veteran calls the line back when it wavers.

**Pikes against horse.** Halt pikemen facing the riders, and the riders' charge breaks on them. Marching pikes are no defence.

**Spread out against blasts.** Against grenadiers, wizards, dragons and catapults, fight **Loose**, and **Scatter** when something is in the air.

**Mind your arrows.** Missed arrows fall on whoever is under them. Shoot at the enemy before the lines meet, and at his reserves after.

**Use the force march.** A squad caught in the wrong fight will keep answering blows; a force march pulls it out (it gets two seconds to break off), or runs it past a fight to where it is needed.

**In a siege, know which rule you are under.** In the set siege (Skirmish) you do not have to kill the garrison: break the gate, put more men round the flagpole than they have, and hold them there; fliers over the wall can get to the courtyard first. In the Conquest there is no flagpole to count: the town is yours when your banner has risen, and it rises only while no army but yours has a man inside the outer wall (chapter [](#capture-point)).

## Quality and quantity

The dice, by which the computer lords reckon a fight before they attack (chapter [](#dice)), favour quality: a strength-3 army rolls low, and may be hit, nine times in ten against a strength-9 one, which rolls low only three times in ten; so the lords think twice before a few strong armies and little of many weak ones. The battlefield favours quality too, but less simply: a giant dies to twelve light infantry who get round him; a dragon to every archer on the field. An army of strong armies, led by a hero with a command artifact, blessed, with a flier and a special army in it, is very hard to stop by any means. A typical A-team: a hero with his artifacts, a wizard, a griffin or two, and heavy cavalry or giants to make up the eight.
