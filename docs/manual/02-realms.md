# The Eight Realms {#realms}

::: lore
*"There were High Lords in Illyria before there were kings, and there will be High Lords when the last crown has gone into the Heartmere."*

— carved over the gate of the Drowned Abbey
:::

Illyria is an island: a long, green land between the Sunderwave Sea in the west and the white wall of the Ironveil in the east, eleven kilometres from the Thornspine in the north to the Dragonback in the south. Rivers divide it. The Aldwater comes down from the northern coast and runs the whole length of the land; the Greymere leaves it and runs west to the sea, shutting off the north-west; below Kingsbridge it forks again into the Westrun and the Sorrow. Where the great rivers meet lies the Heartmere, a broad lake, and in the Heartmere lies Abbey Isle, reached only by the Spit, a causeway of stones barely wide enough for a cart.

For three hundred years Illyria had one throne. The High Lords, eight great houses and peoples, each held a realm and owed the throne a sword, and the throne in its turn kept the peace between them: it judged their quarrels at the Abbey, and it held the eighty walled cities of the land in trust, every one with its own castellan and its own small garrison, so that no lord could march on another without first breaking the king's peace at a dozen gates.

The last king of that line died without an heir in the winter the Heartmere froze. The abbots who kept the throne's records drowned when the ice broke under the Spit, and with them went the roll that said which lord should follow. The castellans closed their gates and waited to see who would come. The eight lords gathered their banners.

Your object is simple to state: **be the last lord standing**, or hold so many of the eighty cities that the others bow to you (chapter [](#lords)). There is more than one way to get there. Brute force is one: a full stack of heavy cavalry on a road is a fearsome thing. But walls will cost you more than any army, every soldier you lose is weeks of a city's work, and the rare beasts and allies of the land are never trained at all: each one that falls is gone for good. Heroes who search the ruins come back with gold, with blades that make an army fight above its strength, or with dragons. And when two armies meet you may fight the battle yourself, soldier by soldier, on the very ground where they met.

## The realms and their lords {#the-realms}

The land is roughly divided between the eight lords, each with a capital castle, a hero and a war chest. Their colours are those of their banners on the map.

### The Aurelian Throne <span class="swatch" style="background:#f4f1e8"></span> {#aurelian-throne}

The knights of the central realm, who held the old king's household and believe the throne is theirs by right of having stood nearest it. Their capital, **Kingshold**, stands west of Kingsbridge in the heart of the land, within a long ride of half a dozen neutral towns and with an enemy on every side. They raise heavy cavalry and heavy infantry, and they are one of the three realms that still keep the craft of the siege engine. A flight of pegasi rides with their lord: the king's own messengers, taken into the war. White is their colour. In the game as it is today, **you play the Aurelian Throne**.

### The Highcrag Giants <span class="swatch" style="background:#d9b13b"></span> {#highcrag-giants}

Giants of the north-west heights, twice and three times the height of a man, who remember a time before the rivers had names and regard the small folk below as weather: unpleasant, and passing. Their castle, **Highcrag**, overlooks the Darkwood from the edge of the Thornspine, and the Greymere cuts them off from the rest of Illyria. They are the poorest of the lords and train only heavy infantry and pikemen of the hill clans that serve them; but two giants march beside their lord, and there are more in the hills. Gold is their colour.

### The Ironhold Dwarves <span class="swatch" style="background:#c8382a"></span> {#ironhold-dwarves}

The dwarves of the southern mountains, under the Dragonback. **Deepforge** is the richest treasury in Illyria, and the dwarves intend to make it richer: every mine and quarry of the land, they hold, was dwarf work once. They train the dwarven legion and heavy infantry, and their engineers build siege engines. Their lord rides a griffin and keeps a company of grenadiers, whose satchel charges are the only thing in the land that likes a wall. Red is their colour.

### The Orcs of Grath <span class="swatch" style="background:#e07a22"></span> {#orcs-of-grath}

Orc clans of the eastern spurs, under the Frostfang. **Grathmaw** is less a city than a camp that grew walls. The other lords call the orcs savages and hire them when they can; the orcs have noticed. They breed wolves and ride them, train warriors with sword and shield, and keep berserkers who fight without shields and without fear. A minotaur walks with their lord. Orange is their colour.

### The Sylvaran Elves <span class="swatch" style="background:#3c9a45"></span> {#sylvaran-elves}

The elves of the Northwood, east of the upper Aldwater. **Sylvaris** is a city of halls among the trees. The elves are archers before they are anything else, and their light horse are the quickest riders in the north. They believe the land would be better for fewer people on it, and say so. Unicorns and pegasi come to their lord's call. Green is their colour.

### The Sunreach Riders <span class="swatch" style="background:#b88d5a"></span> {#sunreach-riders}

Horse lords of the Valdor plains in the south-west, where the land is open from the Ashen Hills to the sea. **Sunreach** is a fortress built round a horse fair. The riders train light and heavy cavalry and little else, and they keep war elephants brought over the sea long ago, which they will not explain. Tan is their colour.

### The Moonward Order <span class="swatch" style="background:#3a64c8"></span> {#moonward-order}

A mystic order of Eastreach, keepers of the old learning, who claim the throne's records were never lost but merely mislaid in their library. **Moonhaven** stands on the eastern shore of the Heartmere, a short march from the Spit. The order trains archers and pikemen; its true strength is its wizards, whose fireballs burst among close-packed troops, and its journeymen, healers who mend the wounded in battle. Blue is their colour.

### Lord Malvoren <span class="swatch" style="background:#222"></span> {#lord-malvoren}

A dark lord of the south-east, whose castle **Dreadspire** rises under the Cinder Spur. No one now living remembers a time before Malvoren, and no one knows what he is. His armies are made of whatever will serve him: heavy infantry, wolf riders, siege engines, a minotaur, and the giant spiders of the eastern woods, which follow him for reasons best not asked. Black is his colour.

## The neutral cities

Seventy-two of the eighty cities belong to no lord when the war begins. Each keeps its own garrison behind its walls, one to three armies of watchmen, archers and heavy foot, more where the walls are stronger. A garrison holds its town and never marches out; but it fights whoever comes to its gates, and a town is not taken by beating its garrison alone. You must then hold it while your banner rises (chapter [](#banners)).

::: tip "The castellans"
A neutral garrison is weak, and in the first weeks of the war the race for the neutral towns is the whole game. Every lord is running it, and the computer lords run it well.
:::

## Two layers, one war

*The High Lords of Illyria* is played on the campaign map, a continent seen from the sky with its roads, rivers, forests and mountains, its cities, its ruins and its temples. Time passes in campaign days; armies march, cities train, gold comes in and goes out.

When your stack marches into an enemy, the campaign stops and asks how the battle is to be fought. **Resolve** settles it as *Warlords* did, with the dice, in a moment. **Fight it** opens the real battle on that exact ground: the camera comes down out of the sky to the field where the stacks met, and every army in them is there, soldier by soldier, with its numbers, its veterans and its heroes. Win, lose or withdraw, the survivors go back onto the map with their kills and their new ranks.

The battle can also be played on its own: the game keeps two set battles, the ford at Oakhaven and the siege of the town beyond it, to learn the field on (chapter [](#quick-start)).
