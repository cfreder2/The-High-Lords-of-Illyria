# The Eight Realms {#realms}

::: lore
*"There were High Lords in Illyria before there were kings, and there will be High Lords when the last crown has gone into the Heartmere."*

— carved over the gate of the Drowned Abbey
:::

Illyria is an island: a long, green land between the Sunderwave Sea in the west and the white wall of the Ironveil in the east, three and a half kilometres from the Thornspine in the north to the Dragonback in the south. Rivers divide it. The Aldwater comes down from the northern coast and runs the whole length of the land; the Greymere leaves it and runs west to the sea, shutting off the north-west; below Kingsbridge it forks again into the Westrun and the Sorrow. Where the great rivers meet lies the Heartmere, a broad lake, and in the Heartmere lies Abbey Isle, reached only by the Spit, a causeway of stones barely wide enough for a cart.

For three hundred years Illyria had one throne. The High Lords, eight great houses and peoples, each held a realm and owed the throne a sword, and the throne in its turn kept the peace between them: it judged their quarrels at the Abbey, and it held the eighty places of the land in trust, the lords' own seats, the walled towns at the fords and passes and the open villages between them, every one with its own castellan or headman and its own small garrison, so that no lord could march on another without first breaking the king's peace at a dozen gates and greens.

The last king of that line died without an heir in the winter the Heartmere froze. The abbots who kept the throne's records drowned when the ice broke under the Spit, and with them went the roll that said which lord should follow. The castellans closed their gates and waited to see who would come. The eight lords gathered their banners.

Your object is simple to state: **be the last lord standing**, or hold so many of the eighty places that the others bow to you (chapter [](#lords)). There is more than one way to get there. Brute force is one: a full stack of heavy cavalry on a road is a fearsome thing. But walls will cost you more than any army, every soldier you lose is weeks of a city's work, and the rare beasts and allies of the land are never trained at all: each one that falls is gone for good. Heroes who search the ruins come back with gold, with blades that make an army fight above its strength, or with dragons. And when two armies meet you may fight the battle yourself, soldier by soldier, on the very ground where they met.

## The realms and their lords {#the-realms}

Each realm has **its own country**, the kind of land its people were bred on. There its soldiers strike a little harder (8%) and march quicker (15%), wherever in Illyria that kind of land is found. Fight a lord on his own ground only when you must.

| Realm | Its country |
|---|---|
| The Aurelian Throne | the open country: level fields and the roads |
| The Highcrag Giants | the hills: steep ground and bare rock |
| The Ironhold Dwarves | the hills |
| The Orcs of Grath | the fens and the hills |
| The Sylvaran Elves | the woods |
| The Sunreach Riders | the open country |
| The Moonward Order | the water's edge (sand, shallows, fords, bridges) and the woods |
| Lord Malvoren | the fens and the dark woods |

The land is divided evenly between the eight lords. Each has a capital castle, a hero, a war chest, and ten places in his own country: his seat, and round it nine that start neutral, three walled towns (those that hold the crossings and passes of his country among them) and six villages, or a village more and a town fewer where there was no room for walls. Their colours are those of their soldiers on the map, and of the banners that fly over their towns.

Every capital trains light infantry and its realm's own kinds, at twice the pace of a town; the rare troops that march with each lord are never trained at all, and each that falls is gone for good (chapter [](#armies)).

| Realm | Capital | Gold | The capital trains (besides light infantry) | Rare troops with the lord |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Aurelian Throne | Kingshold | 300 | heavy cavalry, heavy infantry, catapults | pegasi |
| The Highcrag Giants | Highcrag | 200 | heavy infantry, pikemen | giants, twice |
| The Ironhold Dwarves | Deepforge | 450 | dwarf grenadiers, heavy infantry, catapults | griffins, twice |
| The Orcs of Grath | Grathmaw | 250 | wolf riders, warriors | minotaurs, berserkers |
| The Sylvaran Elves | Sylvaris | 250 | archers, light cavalry | unicorns, pegasi |
| The Sunreach Riders | Sunreach | 300 | light cavalry, heavy cavalry | elephants |
| The Moonward Order | Moonhaven | 300 | archers, pikemen | wizards, journeymen |
| Lord Malvoren | Dreadspire | 350 | heavy infantry, wolf riders, catapults | spiders, minotaurs |

Besides these every lord starts with a company of light infantry and a guard of archers and heavy infantry for his capital's walls and gate, all in one stack with him in the courtyard.

### The Aurelian Throne <span class="swatch" style="background:#f4f1e8"></span> {#aurelian-throne}

The knights of the central realm, who held the old king's household and believe the throne is theirs by right of having stood nearest it. Their capital, **Kingshold**, stands west of Kingsbridge in the heart of the land, by the Elderwood and on the great road from the western sea to the Ironveil, with an enemy on every side. The open fields and the roads are their country. They raise heavy cavalry and heavy infantry, and they are one of the three realms that still keep the craft of the siege engine. A flight of pegasi rides with their lord: the king's own messengers, taken into the war. White is their colour. The game offers the Aurelian Throne first when you choose a lord, and it is a fair realm to learn on.

### The Highcrag Giants <span class="swatch" style="background:#d9b13b"></span> {#highcrag-giants}

Giants of the north-west heights, twice and three times the height of a man, who remember a time before the rivers had names and regard the small folk below as weather: unpleasant, and passing. Their castle, **Highcrag**, overlooks the Darkwood from the edge of the Thornspine, and the Greymere cuts them off from the rest of Illyria. They are the poorest of the lords and train only heavy infantry and pikemen of the hill clans that serve them; but two bands of giants march beside their lord, and there are more in the hills. Steep ground and bare rock are their country. Gold is their colour.

### The Ironhold Dwarves <span class="swatch" style="background:#c8382a"></span> {#ironhold-dwarves}

The dwarves of the south-west, under the Dragonback, where the south road climbs to its pass. **Deepforge** is the richest treasury in Illyria, and the dwarves intend to make it richer: every mine and quarry of the land, they hold, was dwarf work once. The hills are their country. They train grenadiers and heavy infantry, and their engineers build siege engines. Two flights of griffin riders go with their lord. Their grenadiers throw fire and lay satchel charges, the only thing in the land that likes a wall. Red is their colour.

### The Orcs of Grath <span class="swatch" style="background:#e07a22"></span> {#orcs-of-grath}

Orc clans of the north-east, in the broken country between the Frostfang Spur and the Ungoeth Mountains. **Grathmaw** is less a city than a camp that grew walls. The other lords call the orcs savages and hire them when they can; the orcs have noticed. The fens and the hills are their country. They breed wolves and ride them and train warriors with sword and shield; berserkers, who fight without shields and without fear, come to them but are never trained. Minotaurs and a band of berserkers walk with their lord. Orange is their colour.

### The Sylvaran Elves <span class="swatch" style="background:#3c9a45"></span> {#sylvaran-elves}

The elves of the Longwood, the long forest of the south, between Longmere and the Sorrow. **Sylvaris** is a city of halls among the trees at the wood's eastern edge. The woods are their country, any woods. The elves are archers before they are anything else, and they keep light horse besides. They believe the land would be better for fewer people on it, and say so. Unicorns and pegasi come to their lord's call. Green is their colour.

### The Sunreach Riders <span class="swatch" style="background:#b88d5a"></span> {#sunreach-riders}

Horse lords of the western shore, where the land lies open between the Greymere and the Westrun, from the Graycrown to the sea. **Sunreach** is a fortress built round a horse fair, near the western end of the great road. The open country is theirs, as it is the Throne's. The riders train light and heavy cavalry and little else, and they keep war elephants brought over the sea long ago, which they will not explain. Tan is their colour.

### The Moonward Order <span class="swatch" style="background:#3a64c8"></span> {#moonward-order}

A mystic order of Eastreach, keepers of the old learning, who claim the throne's records were never lost but merely mislaid in their library. **Moonhaven** stands east of the Heartmere, a short march from the Spit. The water's edge and the woods are its country: its soldiers are at home on a ford or a bridge. The order trains archers and pikemen; its true strength is its wizards, whose fireballs burst among close-packed troops, and its journeymen, healers who mend the wounded in battle. Blue is their colour.

### Lord Malvoren <span class="swatch" style="background:#222"></span> {#lord-malvoren}

A dark lord of the south-east, whose castle **Dreadspire** rises at the edge of the Eastwood, south of the Cinder Spur and east of Sorrow Ford. The fens and the dark woods are his country. No one now living remembers a time before Malvoren, and no one knows what he is. His armies are made of whatever will serve him: heavy infantry, wolf riders, siege engines, minotaurs, and the giant spiders of the eastern woods, which follow him for reasons best not asked. Black is his colour.

## The neutral cities

Seventy-two of the eighty places belong to no lord when the war begins: 21 walled towns and 51 open villages. (So does the capital of any lord you chose not to face, with five armies behind its walls.)

| Place | Its own men |
|---|---|
| Walled town | three to five armies, as many as its walls are strong: archers, light infantry and archers again, then heavy infantry, then pikemen |
| Village | one levy on its green, light infantry or pikemen |

A garrison holds its place and never marches out; but it fights whoever comes, and a place is not taken by beating its garrison alone. You must then stand in it while your banner rises (chapter [](#banners)).

::: tip "The castellans"
A village's levy is weak, and in the first weeks of the war the race for the villages is the whole game: every lord is running it, and the computer lords run it well. The walled towns are another matter. Their bowmen stand on the walk over the gate, and a lord who comes without engines or grenadiers pays for every plank of it.
:::

## Two layers, one war

*The High Lords of Illyria* is played on the Conquest map, a continent seen from the sky with its roads, rivers, forests and mountains, its cities, its ruins and its temples. Time passes in Conquest days; armies march, cities train, gold comes in and goes out.

When your stack marches into an enemy, nothing stops and nothing asks. The armies fight where they meet, on the map, with every man in them stepped by the battle's own rules, and the camera need not move: zoom in to see the men, out to see each of them as a dot that flashes while he fights. There are no dice and no battle screen; the survivors are simply who is left standing, with their kills and their new ranks.

The battle can also be played on its own: the game keeps two set battles, the ford at Oakhaven and the siege of the town beyond it, to learn the field on (chapter [](#quick-start)).
