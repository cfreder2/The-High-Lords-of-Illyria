# Target Audience — Simulated Personas

> These are **simulated** personas: informed guesses about who this game would attract, written to stress-test the design. They are not market research. Validate the top assumptions with real players (an itch.io prototype, a Discord, a Steam page wishlist test) before betting on them.

Design under test: a seamless continent you can zoom out of as a god (armies look like ants) and into until individual arrows, shields and bodies are physically simulated; Warlords-style factions, cities and named squads; Myth-style physics combat; web-first on itch.io, Steam later.

**Appeal scale:** ★☆☆☆☆ (would bounce) to ★★★★★ (would wishlist on sight).

---

### 1. Dale, 47 — The Myth Veteran
Played *Myth: The Fallen Lords* and *Myth II* online in 1998, still visits Project Magma forums.
- **Hooks:** Dwarven satchel charges bouncing down hills; arrows that hit your own men; gibs that stay on the field; terrain height mattering.
- **Turn-offs:** Anything that feels like "Total War with sprites." Unit counts so big that individual soldiers stop mattering. No multiplayer.
- **Wants:** Ranked team multiplayer, replays (Myth's film files), the feeling that one well-placed fireball changes a battle.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★★ if the zoomed-in combat genuinely plays like Myth.

### 2. Margaret, 56 — The Warlords Loyalist
Played *Warlords II Deluxe* on DOS for years; still plays it in DOSBox. Prefers to think, not click.
- **Hooks:** The continent, cities, heroes, temples, the 8-stack, building an empire over an evening.
- **Turn-offs:** Real-time pressure on the campaign map. Being forced to micro every skirmish.
- **Wants:** Pause, or a slow strategic clock in single-player; auto-resolve for battles she doesn't care about; a strong AI to play against alone.
- **Appeal:** ★★★☆☆ — rises to ★★★★★ with real-time-with-pause and auto-resolve.

### 3. Raj, 34 — The Total War Campaigner
Hundreds of hours in *Rome II* and *Warhammer III*.
- **Hooks:** Campaign map consequences flowing into battles; seeing his named veteran units survive (or not).
- **Turn-offs:** The loading-screen split between campaign and battle (he'd love seamless); thin diplomacy.
- **Wants:** Sieges with walls that physically break; faction variety; meaningful army composition.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆ — the seamless zoom is the thing Total War never gave him.

### 4. Kenji, 29 — The Strategic-Zoom Fan
Plays *Beyond All Reason* and still talks about *Supreme Commander*.
- **Hooks:** Scroll-wheel from a strategic icon view to ground level. Watching a front line move across a whole continent.
- **Turn-offs:** Icons that lie (a unit's icon at god-zoom must reflect real state); input lag in multiplayer.
- **Wants:** Strategic icons and overlays at god-zoom, multi-front warfare, fast and clear information at every zoom level.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★★

### 5. Tyler, 22 — The Spectacle Seeker
Watches *Ultimate Epic Battle Simulator* and huge-battle YouTube compilations.
- **Hooks:** Ten thousand soldiers colliding; slow-motion zoom into a single shield deflecting an arrow.
- **Turn-offs:** Slow economy before anything happens; low unit counts.
- **Wants:** A sandbox or skirmish mode with huge armies, a free camera and slow motion.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆ — high, but fickle; he is a viral amplifier, not a long-term player.

### 6. Zoe, 19 — The itch.io Browser
Browses itch.io for free and pay-what-you-want browser games between classes.
- **Hooks:** Click → playing in under 30 seconds. A striking screenshot. Novelty.
- **Turn-offs:** Big downloads, long tutorials, anything that doesn't run on her school laptop.
- **Wants:** A 10-minute scenario that shows the best moment immediately.
- **Appeal:** ★★★☆☆ — critical for discovery and word of mouth, low long-term commitment.

### 7. Mike, 41 — The Time-Starved Parent
Played RTS in his 20s; now has 45-minute windows after the kids sleep.
- **Hooks:** Deep strategy he can chip away at; nostalgia for both source games.
- **Turn-offs:** Matches that can't be saved; multiplayer that demands 3-hour sessions.
- **Wants:** Save anywhere, pause, single-player campaign, asynchronous or short multiplayer.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆ if save/pause exist.

### 8. Ana, 26 — The Competitive RTS Player
Top-percent ladder player in *Age of Empires II*.
- **Hooks:** Skill expression through positioning, flanks, high ground and dodging.
- **Turn-offs:** Randomness that decides fights; physics chaos she can't control; desyncs; balance neglect.
- **Wants:** Fair, deterministic netcode; readable outcomes; a ranked 1v1 or 2v2 mode on smaller maps.
- **Appeal:** ★★☆☆☆ — the continent scale isn't for her, but a tight skirmish mode could convert her.

### 9. Sam, 31 — The Emergent-Story Grand Strategist
Plays *Crusader Kings III* and *Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord* for the stories.
- **Hooks:** *The Grey Watch*, a squad with a history: battles fought, kills, the day they held a bridge.
- **Turn-offs:** Units as anonymous numbers.
- **Wants:** Squad histories, heroes with traits, rivalries, a chronicle of the campaign.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★★ — the named-unit system is exactly her hook.

### 10. Ivo, 37 — The Simulation Storyteller
*Dwarf Fortress* and *RimWorld* devotee.
- **Hooks:** Persistent wounds and mutilation; veterans who limp; a battlefield that remembers.
- **Turn-offs:** Systems that reset; shallow simulation.
- **Wants:** Soldiers who carry injuries between battles; memorials to fallen squads; legends.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆

### 11. Priya, 28 — The Modder and Map Maker
Made custom maps for *Myth II* as a teenager; now a software engineer.
- **Hooks:** A custom engine with open, readable data formats.
- **Turn-offs:** Locked-down content; no tools.
- **Wants:** Map editor, unit definitions in text files, scriptable scenarios, Steam Workshop later.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆ — modders keep games alive for decades (Myth is proof).

### 12. Jordan, 33 — The Retro Aesthetic Fan
Loves the "boomer shooter" revival and HD-2D games.
- **Hooks:** Sprite soldiers in a modern-lit 3D world; the late-90s look done right.
- **Turn-offs:** Generic Unity-store realism; muddy art.
- **Wants:** Strong art direction, crunchy sound, chunky gore.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆ — the hybrid sprite style is a marketing asset, not just a performance trick.

### 13. The Discord Crew — four friends, 24–27
Play co-op games together on weekends.
- **Hooks:** Each friend runs a front of the same war; one zooms in to save another's battle.
- **Turn-offs:** Only competitive multiplayer; complicated hosting.
- **Wants:** 2–4 player co-op campaign against AI factions, drop-in links, voice-friendly pacing.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★★ — the seamless shared continent is a natural co-op game.

### 14. Lena, 27 — The Streamer
Variety streamer with a strategy-leaning audience.
- **Hooks:** Moments chat can react to: a friendly-fire disaster, a last stand, a dramatic zoom-in.
- **Turn-offs:** Long dead time; visuals unreadable on a stream.
- **Wants:** Replays, a cinematic camera, a "zoom to the most interesting fight" button, chat-vote events.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆

### 15. Diego, 20 — The Budget-Laptop / Steam Deck Player
Student with an Intel-graphics laptop and a Steam Deck.
- **Hooks:** A great-looking strategy game that actually runs on his hardware.
- **Turn-offs:** Stutter, battery drain, tiny UI text, mouse-only controls.
- **Wants:** Stable 60 fps, scalable settings, controller support, readable UI at 800p.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆ — the performance budget in the design doc is for him.

### 16. Owen, 17 — The Physics Sandbox Tinkerer
Plays *Teardown*, *Besiege* and *People Playground*.
- **Hooks:** Knocking a siege tower into a river and watching what happens.
- **Turn-offs:** Scripted destruction.
- **Wants:** Destructible walls, explosives, a sandbox mode with spawnable units.
- **Appeal:** ★★★★☆

### 17. Harold, 63 — The Retired Wargamer
Played board wargames for decades; now plays *Combat Mission* and *Unity of Command*.
- **Hooks:** Ballistics that obey physics; terrain and elevation mattering; a believable chain of command.
- **Turn-offs:** Fast clicking; small text; arcade feel.
- **Wants:** Pause and give orders; UI scaling; detailed unit information; historical-feeling logistics.
- **Appeal:** ★★★☆☆ — rises with pause, readability and depth.

### 18. Chloe, 25 — The Fantasy Lore Reader
Reads epic fantasy; plays games for their worlds.
- **Hooks:** The High Lords, the temples, the history of Illyria.
- **Turn-offs:** Lore-free sandboxes.
- **Wants:** A narrative single-player campaign, faction stories, a reason to care which banner wins.
- **Appeal:** ★★★☆☆ — depends on writing that doesn't exist yet.

## What the personas agree on

| Signal | Personas | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **The seamless god-to-ground zoom is the headline feature** | 3, 4, 5, 13, 14 | It is the trailer, the store capsule and the reason to wishlist. Prove it first. |
| **Named squads with persistent histories** | 1, 9, 10, 18 | Cheap to build, high emotional payoff. Lean in: chronicles, scars, memorials. |
| **Physics chaos is the soul of combat** | 1, 5, 14, 16 | Friendly fire, debris and gore must survive into the final design. |
| **Pause / slow time in single-player** | 2, 7, 17 | Real-time continent for multiplayer; real-time-with-pause for single-player. |
| **Auto-resolve and delegation** | 2, 7, 4 | Nobody can micro nine fronts. Let players delegate fronts to AI generals. |
| **Co-op on a shared continent** | 13, 7, 14 | Co-op vs AI may be a stronger multiplayer pitch than competitive. |
| **Runs on weak hardware, instantly** | 6, 15 | Validates web-first and the 60 fps laptop budget. |
| **Competitive ladder players are a poor fit for the main mode** | 8 | Offer a small-map skirmish mode later; don't design around them. |

## Recommended design responses

1. **Seamless continent with real-time-with-pause** in single-player, real-time in multiplayer (answers Open Question Q2 in the design doc).
2. **AI generals** that fight fronts you aren't watching, so attention, not micro, is the scarce resource.
3. **"Hot spot" camera button:** jump to the most dramatic fight in progress.
4. **Squad chronicle:** every squad keeps a log of battles, kills, losses and honours.
5. **Sandbox mode** with huge armies and a free camera: cheap marketing and a playground for the physics.
6. **First public prototype:** one region, two factions, zoom from map to arrows. Ship it on itch.io to test personas 4, 5, 6 and 13.
